# Outbound Voice Rules

Applies to every email and LinkedIn draft. The goal: messages that read like a sharp human rep wrote them after 20 minutes of real research — because that's exactly what the agent did.

## Structure

**Email (under 90 words, 4 parts):**
1. **Signal-led opener** — the specific, sourced thing that made this account worth contacting, in one sentence. Not flattery; evidence. ("Saw the Series B last week — congrats. New board seats usually come with a D&O requirement in the term sheet.")
2. **Bridge** — one sentence connecting the signal to the pain Corgi removes.
3. **Proof** — one line from `positioning.md`, matched to the prospect's stage/vertical.
4. **CTA** — one low-friction ask. Interest-based ("worth a look?") over calendar demands.

**LinkedIn (under 60 words, or under 280 chars for a connection note):** same logic, more casual, no proof paragraph — signal + one-line value + soft ask.

## Rules

- **Subject lines:** 2-5 words, lowercase ok, reference the signal ("your series b", "d&o before close"). Never clickbait, never "Quick question".
- **One signal per message.** The best evidence leads; save the rest for follow-ups (draft a 2-touch sequence when signals support it).
- **Write simply.** Short sentences. No jargon the prospect's own site doesn't use. Read it aloud; if it sounds like a template, rewrite it.
- **Banned phrases:** "I hope this finds you well", "I wanted to reach out", "quick question", "touching base", "circle back", "I know you're busy", "game-changer", "revolutionize", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "I was impressed by".
- **No em dashes** in outbound copy; use commas or periods.
- **Never fake familiarity.** Don't pretend to have used their product or met them.
- **Never trash competitors by name.** Contrast with "the broker process", not "Vouch".
- **Personalization must be load-bearing.** If the personalized line could be deleted without changing the message, it's decoration; cut it or find a better signal.
- **Honest sourcing.** Only reference what's publicly visible. Nothing that implies surveillance ("I saw you viewed...").

## Tone

Peer-to-peer, founder-literate, direct. Corgi's brand is "built for founders by founders" and "speed of compute": messages should themselves feel fast and unbureaucratic. Confident, never pushy; it's fine to say "if this isn't on your plate, who should I ask?"

## Feedback loop

Before drafting, read `context/corgi/feedback.md`. After any human edits a draft or reports reply data, append what changed and why. Patterns that repeat 2+ times graduate into rules in THIS file.
